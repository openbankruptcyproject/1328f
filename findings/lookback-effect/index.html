The § 1328(f) Lookback Effect — Pre/Post BAPCPA

Section 1328(f) was added to the Bankruptcy Code in 2005 by BAPCPA. Before BAPCPA there was no statutory bar on successive Chapter 13 discharges based on a recent prior discharge. This page examines the empirical effect of the rule's introduction.

Pre-BAPCPA (before October 2005)

Under prior law, a debtor could chain successive Chapter 13 cases with no statutory lookback. Courts had some discretion under "good faith" doctrine to police abusive successive filings, but the constraint was discretionary, not categorical.

The empirical pattern pre-BAPCPA: successive Ch.13 filings were common but not viewed as systematically problematic. The 4/5-plan-completion rate was already low, so successive filings often represented attempts by struggling debtors to make a second attempt rather than abuse.

Post-BAPCPA (October 2005 onward)

The rule's effect on filing behavior has been mixed:

  • Sophisticated filers (those with attorneys) generally screen for the lookback at intake. Successive Ch.13 filing where the bar applies dropped substantially in attorney-represented cases.
  • Pro-se filers have been less affected, because pro-se debtors often don't know the rule exists.
  • Plan completion + no discharge remains common: debtors who file pro-se Ch.13 with a recent prior discharge often complete the plan without realizing the lookback bars discharge until the very end.

The 2-year vs. 4-year asymmetry

The rule has different lookback periods for prior chapters:

  • Prior Ch.7/11/12 discharge: 4-year lookback (longer because those discharges are typically full discharges of unsecured debt)
  • Prior Ch.13 discharge: 2-year lookback (shorter because Ch.13 discharges typically follow 3-5 years of plan payments)

The asymmetry reflects Congress's view that Ch.13 plan completion already builds in a "cooling off" period through the plan duration itself.

Procedural reform

The Advisory Committee on Bankruptcy Rules accepted 26-BK-3 in March 2026 — a proposed amendment to Bankruptcy Rule 4004 to require § 1328(f) screening at the petition stage. The aim is to catch lookback-barred cases at intake rather than at discharge, reducing the population of debtors who complete plans without learning of the bar until the end.

The proposed rule is in the public-comment phase. The Open Bankruptcy Project's analysis was cited in the committee record (Tab 4C, p. 125 fn. 4).
