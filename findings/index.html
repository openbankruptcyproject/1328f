Empirical Findings

Headline statistics from the § 1328(f) successive-filing dataset. All figures will be refreshed quarterly as new BAPCPA data becomes available and as PACER pulls expand the docket-level coverage.

National scope

The § 1328(f) lookback applies to every Chapter 13 case filed since BAPCPA (October 2005). The total Ch.13 filing pool from 2005-2026 is approximately 8 million cases. Of those, an estimated 12-18% had a prior bankruptcy filing somewhere in the lookback window — though only a fraction of that subset triggers the actual discharge bar (because many priors were dismissed without discharge).

Where lookback bites hardest

Several patterns stand out from preliminary district-level analysis:

  • High-volume Ch.13 districts (Southeast: GA, AL, NC, SC, TN) show high rates of successive filing because debtors who don't complete one plan often try again.
  • Pro-se filers are disproportionately caught — they often don't know the rule exists. Many districts' pro-se desks add lookback-screening to the petition checklist after seeing the pattern.
  • Conversion paths matter: Ch.7-to-Ch.13 conversion creates a discharge that does count for lookback purposes, while Ch.13-to-Ch.7 conversion is treated under different rules.

The "completed but no discharge" outcome

One of the more interesting empirical findings is that many debtors who would be subject to § 1328(f) still complete their Chapter 13 plan (3-5 years of payments) before the lookback issue is raised at the discharge stage. The plan still confers tangible benefits:

  • The automatic stay protected payments throughout the plan
  • Secured-debt cures (mortgage arrears, etc.) were performed
  • Tax-debt payments may have satisfied non-dischargeable obligations
  • Income-driven payment structure provided breathing room

But the unsecured debt remaining at plan completion is not discharged. Whether the case was "worth it" is a fact-specific judgment depending on the debtor's actual debt mix.
