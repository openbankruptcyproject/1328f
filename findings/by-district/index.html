§ 1328(f) Patterns by District

Successive-filing rates and lookback-bar incidence vary substantially across the 90 federal bankruptcy districts. This page tracks per-district patterns based on BAPCPA Table 6 + targeted PACER pulls.

The geography of successive Chapter 13 filings

Successive Ch.13 filings are concentrated in a small number of high-volume districts. The pattern correlates with overall Ch.13 filing rates: districts with the most Chapter 13 cases also have the most successive filings, both in absolute numbers and as a fraction of total filings.

Patterns observed in preliminary analysis:

  • Southeast circuit (4th, 5th, 11th): highest absolute volume of successive Ch.13 filings. Likely correlates with the South's Chapter-13-heavy filing culture.
  • Northern districts (1st, 2nd, 6th): lower absolute volume but similar percentage rates — debtors there usually file Ch.7 first.
  • Western districts (9th, 10th): mixed; some districts (D. Az., D. Nev.) have moderate Ch.13 share with high successive-filing rates.

Pro-se prevalence by district

The fraction of pro-se Chapter 13 filings (no attorney of record at filing) varies enormously across districts. Districts with high pro-se rates also see disproportionate § 1328(f) lookback issues, because pro-se filers are most likely to be unaware of the rule.

This research will publish per-district pro-se rate + lookback-encounter rate as a paired statistic when the underlying PACER pulls complete.

Filing fee waiver patterns

Districts where Chapter 7 filing fees are routinely waived under § 1930(f)(3) interact with § 1328(f) in a subtle way: a debtor who got a Ch.7 fee waiver and discharge cannot use Ch.13 within 4 years. The fee-waiver path may give the impression that bankruptcy is "easier" than it actually is for repeat filers.
